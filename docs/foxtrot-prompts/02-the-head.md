# The Head

## Prompt

Use the attached pixel-art fox sprite sheet as the character reference for Foxtrot.

Foxtrot is a voxel fox built from chunky cubes — a 16x16 pixel-art sprite extruded faithfully into 3D. Rust-orange body, lighter orange highlight blocks on cheeks and back, cream-white muzzle, chest bib, and tail tip with a grey-white band before it, thick dark charcoal outline cubes on every edge, a single white cube for the eye, dark stubby feet. Hard cube edges throughout, no smoothing, no fur. Warm limited palette: rust, amber, cream, charcoal, grey.

He sits at the edge of a high shelf — or the top of a wide monitor — in his alert pose: upright, both ears straight up, tail raised, head turned sharply toward one spot below. Beneath him, a field of small glowing tiles floats in staggered rows, each one a session: most glow soft cream or cool blue-grey, their surfaces etched with faint lines of code or conversation. One tile pulses amber-red, brighter than the rest, casting a warm flare upward — the session that needs him. Near his muzzle a blocky speech burst reads "YIP!" in chunky pixel font (Press Start 2P), cream letters on a charcoal badge, like a retro game bark.

The mood is a watchtower at dusk. Warm amber light spills from the left, catching the cube edges of his coat; the background falls into deep charcoal and muted blue, the tiles providing most of the scene's illumination. Shallow depth of field: Foxtrot and the troubled tile are sharp, the far tiles softly out of focus. Calm vigilance — everything watched over.

Aspect ratio 4:3. Resolution 2048x1536. Voxel character in a semi-stylized environment, cinematic lighting, no fur texture, no realistic animal features.

## Notes

- If the model drifts toward a realistic fox or soft edges, prepend "no fur, no realistic animal, strictly cubes, Minecraft-adjacent voxel style" near the top of the prompt.
- The tiles should read as abstract UI surfaces, not literal screenshots — faint glyph textures, not legible text. The amber-red one must stand out clearly as the focal counterpart to Foxtrot himself.
- The speech burst is a flat 2D element floating in the 3D scene (like a comic bark, not a 3D object). Keep it small — it should accent, not dominate.
