# The Deck

## Prompt

Use the attached pixel-art fox sprite sheet as the character reference for Foxtrot.

Foxtrot is a voxel fox built from chunky cubes — a 16x16 pixel-art sprite extruded faithfully into 3D. Rust-orange body, lighter orange highlight blocks on the back and cheeks, cream-white muzzle, chest bib and tail tip, a grey-white band before the tail tip, thick dark charcoal outline cubes on every edge (not pure black), a single white cube for the eye, dark stubby feet. Hard cube edges throughout, no smoothing, no fur texture. Warm limited palette: rust, amber, cream, charcoal, grey.

He sits on the bezel of a dark widescreen monitor, centered, viewed from slightly below — eye level with the bottom third of the screen. Behind him the display is filled with the developer's world: a grid of terminal tiles in muted charcoal and slate, each tile holding conversation text or scrolling code in soft monospace greens and ambers. One tile in the lower right pulses with a faint amber glow — something needs attention. The rest are quiet, dim, working. Foxtrot is in his alert pose: sitting up tall, ears pricked, head turned toward the glowing tile as if he just noticed it. His proportions are a small dog's, maybe ten centimeters on the bezel.

A blocky speech burst floats beside his head — "YIP!" in chunky pixel font (Press Start 2P), cream text on a charcoal badge, like a retro game bark.

The room is dark. The only light is the monitor's warm glow washing over the fox and the desk edge beneath him, casting a faint amber-cream reflection on the surface. Late-night coding, quiet, focused. Shallow depth of field: the fox and the nearest tiles are sharp, the far corners of the screen soften. Voxel-art character in a photorealistic environment. 16:9 aspect ratio, 2K resolution, cinematic lighting, 35mm lens.

## Notes

- If the model drifts toward a realistic fox or soft fur, reinforce early: "no fur, no realistic animal, strictly cubes, Minecraft-adjacent voxel style."
- The terminal tiles should read as a developer UI (dark theme, monospace text, subtle grid lines) but stay blurred enough not to distract from the fox — they are context, not content.
- The monitor bezel grounds the fox physically in the scene; if the model places him floating inside the screen instead, specify "sitting ON the physical bezel of the monitor, in front of the display, casting a shadow on the desk."
